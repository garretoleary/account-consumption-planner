## The decision
The Chief Operating Officer is the likely sponsor and proposed final business decision-maker. The Division Operations Director and Shift Handover Lead influence adoption and success criteria; the Enterprise Security Director, Data Governance Lead and Procurement Director can block approval. Confirm this authority map in discovery. The decision should follow an agreed downtime baseline, a credible business case, security clearance and a negotiated pilot order—not the prospect score alone.

Use the executive connection from Talvexon Industrial Systems to request an introduction tied to the published factory downtime efficiency target.

## The commercial shape
Propose a **$10,000–$15,000 minimum commitment per billing month**, credited against usage, for a **minimum term of three billing months**. The total minimum commitment is **$10,000–$15,000 per billing month × three billing months = $30,000–$45,000 over the three-month term**. Charge usage above the monthly credit at the planning rate of **$10 per million tokens consumed in each billing month**.

Keep the initial scope to one division and run the **six-week pilot period inside the three-month minimum term**. Hold the first value review immediately after the full pilot, then make the expansion decision before the third billing month ends. Expand by one division only after operational acceptance, measured benefit and explicit approval from both sides. This commitment is large enough to require attention from both teams while limiting the customer’s exposure before value is demonstrated.

The proven similar case targets reduced production downtime: **110,000 shift handovers per year × $135 production-downtime impact per shift handover in the annual baseline × 10% potential improvement per year = $1,485,000 potential production downtime avoided per year**. Its **4,000,000,000 tokens per month** would cost **$40,000 per month**, or **$480,000 per year**, at the planning rate. These are comparison economics, not Qelvoryn forecasts; delivery costs and local applicability remain untested. A small commitment limits initial exposure while usage-based growth follows adoption.

## What must be true for them to say yes
- The downtime target has an accountable executive owner and a funded improvement path.
- Shift handover gaps materially contribute to downtime; local records support a measurable baseline.
- Operators can use the synthesis within existing handover routines, with human review.
- Customer-approved success thresholds, usage limits and expansion gates are documented before signing.

## Risks to the deal and how each is reduced
- **Security and data approval stalls:** complete the security and data review every large enterprise runs before signing. Our Security Lead answers controls questions; our Privacy Counsel and Solution Architect address retention, access, data flows and contractual handling. Close findings before signature.
- **Similar-case economics do not transfer:** our Value Lead validates shift handover volumes, costs and attributable downtime changes with their Operations Finance Lead.
- **Usage costs outrun benefits:** our Commercial Lead sets billing alerts, a customer-approved cap and explicit expansion approvals.
- **Operators reject the workflow or outputs are unreliable:** our Pilot Lead co-designs handovers with their Shift Handover Lead and requires human validation.
- **The warm route lacks authority:** our Account Lead confirms sponsor access and procurement ownership early.

## What I would walk away from
No accountable sponsor, no usable baseline, unresolved security requirements, demands for guaranteed savings, or uncapped usage without budget ownership. Walk away if the customer will not commit **$10,000–$15,000 per billing month for at least three billing months**, or if either side will not resource the six-week pilot, attend the value review or make the expansion decision before the third billing month ends.
