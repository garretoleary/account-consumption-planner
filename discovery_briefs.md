# Qelvoryn Forgeworks discovery call brief
**DRAFT — all figures, roles and dates are invented.**

## Why you, why now
Qelvoryn Forgeworks’ published factory downtime efficiency target creates a timely reason to examine where production time is being lost. A discovery call could test whether shift handovers are a meaningful constraint on that target before committing resources.

## Value hypothesis
**Hypothesis to test — not a promise.** Shift handover synthesis may help reduce production downtime, drawing on a similar case: **110,000 shift handovers per year × $135 production-downtime impact per shift handover during that year × 10% potential improvement per year = $1,485,000 potential production downtime avoided per year**. The separate plain measure was **5,500 supervisor hours saved per year**; it is a separate result and is not the source of the money value. The prospect’s case volume, impact per shift handover and achievable improvement would need to be established.

## Five discovery questions
1. What is most at risk if you miss the factory downtime efficiency target?
2. What does production downtime currently cost you per year?
3. What have you already tried to reduce downtime, and what happened?
4. Who feels the effects of downtime most directly?
5. What other priorities compete with downtime reduction for attention and resources?

## Likely objection
“We already have shift handover procedures, and changing them could distract teams from production.”

## Honest answer
That is a fair concern: a similar case elsewhere does not prove it would work for you. We would first need to understand whether handover gaps contribute materially to your downtime; if they do not, this may not be worth pursuing.

## Small first step to propose
Propose a six-week pilot in one division, using one agreed measure: production downtime hours per week. Fix the baseline before starting as the average weekly downtime hours over the preceding six weeks, and compare results against it without treating any change as proof of causation.

## My opening

Thank you for making the time. Your new colleague suggested we speak, as we have worked together before. I saw that you have published a target to cut factory downtime. I am not here to present anything. I would like to understand what stands between you and that target today. Then we can decide together whether anything is worth a second conversation.

# Zynthara Biosystems discovery call brief
**DRAFT — all figures, roles and dates are invented.**

## Why you, why now
Zynthara Biosystems faces a distribution compliance deadline in nine months, making this a timely call to explore whether shipment exceptions could put readiness at risk. Discussing current bottlenecks now could help identify what needs attention before that deadline.

## Value hypothesis
**Hypothesis to test — not a promise.** A similar shipment exception resolution case suggests that reducing shipment exception cycle time could create value for Zynthara, subject to testing its volumes, costs and improvement potential.

Complete value working: **95,000 shipment exception cases per year × $100 shipment-delay impact per shipment exception case during that year × 16% potential improvement per year = $1,520,000 potential shipment-delay impact avoided per year.**

Separate plain measure from the similar case: **15,200 shipment delay days avoided per year.** This is a separate result and is not the source of the money value.

## Five discovery questions
1. What is most at risk if distribution compliance readiness slips beyond the deadline?
2. What do shipment exceptions currently cost you in money and delay days per year?
3. What have you already tried to reduce shipment exception cycle time, and what happened?
4. Which teams or people feel the consequences of shipment exceptions most?
5. What other priorities compete for attention before the compliance deadline?

## Likely objection
“We cannot divert capacity from compliance work to an uncertain improvement effort.”

## Honest answer
That is a reasonable concern: a similar case elsewhere does not prove it would work for you. We would need to understand your exception process and compliance constraints before judging whether a bounded test merits the effort.

## Small first step to propose
Propose a four-week pilot in one division, using shipment exception cycle time as the single agreed measure. Fix the baseline before starting as that division’s average cycle time over the preceding four weeks, with the case definition and calculation held constant throughout the pilot.

I would like to hear what worries you most between now and that date. If I can help, I will say so. If I cannot, I will say that too.

# Virelqua Process Labs discovery call brief
**DRAFT — all figures, roles and dates are invented.**

## Why you, why now
Your published batch-release cycle target makes release-review time a timely topic for discussion. A discovery call could clarify whether batch record review puts that target at risk and where reducing delays would matter most.

## Value hypothesis
**Hypothesis to test — not a promise.** A similar batch record review case suggests that reducing release review time could create value, subject to testing your volumes, costs and constraints.

Complete value working: **60,000 batch records reviewed per year × $95 release-review effort per batch record reviewed during that year × 18% potential improvement per year = $1,026,000 potential release-review effort avoided per year.**

The similar case’s separate plain measure was **2,400 reviewer hours saved per quarter**. It is a separate result and is not the source of the money value; neither result establishes your likely outcome.

## Five discovery questions
1. What is most at risk if you miss the batch-release cycle target?
2. What do release-review delays currently cost you in time, money or operational disruption?
3. What have you already tried to reduce release-review time, and what happened?
4. Who feels the effects of release-review delays most directly?
5. What other priorities compete with this target for attention and resources?

## Likely objection
“Our validation obligations may make changing batch record review more effort than the potential time savings justify.”

## Honest answer
That is a legitimate concern: a similar case elsewhere does not prove it would work for you. We would need to understand your validation requirements and measure any benefit against the effort and risk before recommending a broader change.

## Small first step to propose
Subject to your approvals, propose a **six-week pilot in one manufacturing division**, with **reviewer hours per completed batch record** as the single agreed measure. Fix the baseline before starting, using that division’s reviewer hours per completed batch record over the **six weeks immediately preceding the pilot**, with the same scope and measurement rules throughout.

## Compliance, privacy and security questions
- **Validation:** What validation evidence and change-control documentation will you provide? **Our answering role:** Validation Lead. **Your approving role:** Quality Assurance Lead.
- **Data location:** Where will our data be processed and stored? **Our answering role:** Cloud Architecture Lead. **Your approving role:** Data Governance Lead.
- **Access:** Who can access our data, and how will access be authorized and revoked? **Our answering role:** Identity and Access Management Lead. **Your approving role:** Information Security Lead.
- **Retention:** How long will you retain our data, and how will deletion be handled? **Our answering role:** Privacy Lead. **Your approving role:** Records Management Lead.
- **Security:** What safeguards and incident-response procedures will protect our data? **Our answering role:** Security Lead. **Your approving role:** Chief Information Security Officer.
- **Model training:** Will our data be used to train your models? **Our answering role:** AI Governance Lead. **Your approving role:** Data Privacy Lead.

## My opening

Thank you for your time. Your validation partner thought it was worth us talking. I read that you have set a public target for batch release. I am here to listen, not to sell. I would like to understand what slows release today and what you have already tried. At the end, you can tell me whether this was useful.
